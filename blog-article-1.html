Margin vs Markup: The #1 Pricing Mistake Small Businesses Make

By MarginWize Editorial Team • July 2026 • 5 Min Read

Every year, thousands of new retail entrepreneurs, e-commerce sellers, and contractors set their selling prices believing they are securing a 50% profit margin, only to discover at tax time that their actual margin was a meager 33%. What caused this financial discrepancy? They confused Markup with Profit Margin.

The Core Mathematical Difference

While both terms measure profit, their starting baseline (the divisor in the math equation) is fundamentally different:

  • Profit Margin measures profit as a percentage of total revenue (selling price).
  • Markup measures profit as a percentage of item cost (COGS).
Margin (%) = (Profit / Selling Price) × 100
Markup (%) = (Profit / Item Cost) × 100

A Real-World Example: The $50 Pricing Trap

Suppose an online seller purchases a handcrafted ceramic mug from a supplier for $50.00. The seller desires a 50% profit margin to cover shipping, website fees, and advertising.

The Mistake: The seller adds a 50% markup ($25.00) to the $50.00 cost, pricing the mug at $75.00.

The Reality:

  • Markup = ($25.00 profit / $50.00 cost) × 100 = 50% Markup
  • Actual Margin = ($25.00 profit / $75.00 selling price) × 100 = 33.33% Profit Margin

To achieve a true 50% profit margin, the mug must actually be sold for $100.00 ($50 cost + $50 profit)!
