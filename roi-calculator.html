What Is Return on Investment (ROI)?

Return on Investment (ROI) is a high-level financial ratio used to evaluate the efficiency and profitability of an investment or compare the relative returns of multiple capital expenditures. Expressed as a percentage, ROI measures net profit earned relative to the initial capital risked.

Whether analyzing digital ad spend, purchasing manufacturing equipment, acquiring real estate, or purchasing stock equity, ROI provides a universal benchmark for financial performance.

Mathematical ROI Formulas

Net Profit ($) = Final Value - Initial Investment
Basic ROI (%) = (Net Profit / Initial Investment) × 100

Annualized ROI / CAGR (%) = [ (Final Value / Initial Investment)(1 / Years) - 1 ] × 100

Step-by-Step Worked Example: Digital Marketing Campaign

Example: E-Commerce Ad Campaign

A company invests $15,000 into a 3-month digital media campaign, generating $42,000 in direct trackable sales:

Net Gain = $42,000 - $15,000 = $27,000
Total ROI (%) = ($27,000 / $15,000) × 100 = 180.0%
Capital Multiple = $42,000 / $15,000 = 2.80x

3 Critical Considerations When Evaluating ROI

  1. Account for Time Horizon: A 50% basic ROI over 6 months is drastically superior to a 50% ROI accrued over 10 years. Always calculate Annualized ROI (CAGR) for long-term investments.
  2. Include Hidden Opportunity Costs: Factor in administrative labor hours, maintenance costs, and capital financing interest rates when calculating true net investment.
  3. Combine ROI with Cash Flow Analysis: A project may deliver high long-term ROI but lock up cash flow in the short term, creating liquidity risks.
