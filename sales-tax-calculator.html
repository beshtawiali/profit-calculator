Understanding Sales Tax and Value-Added Tax (VAT)

Sales Tax, Goods and Services Tax (GST), and Value-Added Tax (VAT) are consumption taxes levied by national, state, or municipal governments on retail sales of goods and services. While they share the goal of raising public revenue, their implementation differs significantly:

  • Sales Tax (e.g. United States): A single-stage tax assessed strictly at the final point of purchase by the end consumer.
  • VAT / GST (e.g. UK, EU, Canada, Australia): A multi-stage tax collected incrementally at each production stage, where businesses deduct input VAT paid against output VAT collected.

Mathematical Tax Formulas

Adding Tax (Net → Gross):
Tax Amount = Net Amount × (Tax Rate / 100)
Gross Total = Net Amount + Tax Amount = Net Amount × (1 + Tax Rate / 100)

Extracting Tax (Gross → Net):
Net Amount = Gross Total / (1 + Tax Rate / 100)
Tax Amount = Gross Total - Net Amount

Step-by-Step Worked Example

Example: B2B Software Consulting Invoice

A consultancy issues a B2B invoice inclusive of 20% UK VAT with a total paid amount of $1,500.00:

Net Base Price = $1,500.00 / (1 + 0.20) = $1,500.00 / 1.20 = $1,250.00
Extracted VAT Amount = $1,500.00 - $1,250.00 = $250.00

3 Tax Compliance Tips for Business Owners

  1. Always Verify Combined Rates: In the US, state, county, and local city tax rates combine together. Ensure you charge the accurate destination tax rate.
  2. Keep Clear Tax-Exclusive Invoices: B2B customers frequently require itemized invoices listing net prices and tax amounts separately for tax credit filing.
  3. Separate Tax Funds Immediately: Sales tax collected from customers belongs to the tax authority. Keep collected tax in a separate reserve account to prevent cash flow strain at filing time.
