What Is Markup and Why Does It Matter?

Markup is the percentage added to the cost price of a product or service to arrive at its final selling price. Cost-plus markup pricing is one of the most widely used retail and wholesale pricing methodologies due to its mathematical simplicity and guarantee that every sale covers item production costs.

While markup and profit margin both measure profitability, they use completely different base figures in their calculations:

  • Markup uses the item's cost price as the base divisor.
  • Profit Margin uses the item's selling price (revenue) as the base divisor.

Markup Formulas and Calculations

To set your prices using cost-plus markup, use the following core formulas:

Profit Amount ($) = Cost × (Markup % / 100)
Selling Price ($) = Cost + Profit Amount = Cost × (1 + Markup % / 100)

Equivalent Profit Margin (%) = [ Markup % / (100 + Markup %) ] × 100

Step-by-Step Worked Example: Retail Furniture

Example: Custom Oak Dining Chair

A boutique furniture manufacturer produces hand-carved oak chairs:

  • Direct Production Cost: $120.00 per chair
  • Target Wholesale Markup: 80%
Profit Dollars = $120.00 × 0.80 = $96.00
Selling Price = $120.00 + $96.00 = $216.00
Profit Margin = ($96.00 / $216.00) × 100 = 44.44%

Practical Pricing Tips for Business Owners

  1. Understand the 100% Markup Myth: A 100% markup (keystone pricing) doubles your cost to determine price, but yields a 50% profit margin—not a 100% margin.
  2. Account for Overhead Beyond COGS: Ensure your markup percentage is set high enough to cover secondary expenses like rent, marketing, merchant payment fees, and shrinkage.
  3. Use Tiered Markups Across Categories: Fast-moving commodity products can survive on lower markups (e.g. 20-30%), whereas slow-moving specialty items require higher markups (100-200%) to justify storage and handling.
